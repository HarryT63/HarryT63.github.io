How It Plays

Fish Party is designed to be hyper accessible, so that anyone can play regardless of gaming experience. Each player controls a fish with only 1 button: you press it to change the direction your fish rotates, and you can hold it to dash. That’s it.

  • Each fish has a simple goal to score points, and players compete to score the most points totaled up across all the fish.
  • Every player gets a turn on every fish in the environment, ensuring that the games are fair.
  • One button controls allow for 12 players to play locally very easily.
  • Players can share controllers, or have multiple people all pressing a different key on the keyboard.

Real World Ecosystems

Since Fish Party is a game about ecosystems, it was important to me that we accurately represent behaviors of real world fish in the environments they would be living in.

  • I took the lead researching fish in freshwater and saltwater environments to learn about unique behaviors we could include in the game.
  • Every level in the game is based on a real world location, like Bermuda or the Great Barrier Reef.

Examples of real world behaviors

  • Groupers and eels often hunt together in real life, so in Fish Party they share points when they eat prey near each other.
  • Male Bowfin are very protective of their spawn, so in Fish Party one player plays as the parent and protects their children from predators.

Iteration

The current fish in the game have gone through a lot of iteration to make them more fun.

Slow Gameplay

Problem
The initial prototype for the game had interesting interactions between fish, but the moment to moment gameplay of playing as a fish was not very fun. The game felt too slow.
Change
We increased the base speed of all the fish, and added the option to dash. If the player holds their button down, their fish dashes forward.
Result
This adds a new system for players to engage with to allow for more decision making and to make gameplay more exciting.

Blenny vs. Damselfish

The blenny scores points by eating the algae while the damselfish scores points for being near the algae, so naturally these two are enemies.

Problem
In the initial prototype, it was way too easy for blennies to dodge the damselfish and quickly eat the algae, leaving the damselfish without any points.
Change
  • I changed the algae to have 2 hit points, and made the blenny bounce back when they took a bite out of the algae.
  • I made algae grow in groups of 3, so there is more algae for the damselfish to defend and more for the blenny to eat.
  • I gave the damselfish a small amount of points for eating small fish, as well as for being near algae.
Result
This gives the damselfish an extra incentive to eat the blennies and other small fish passing by. Now both fish have a fair way to score and feel exciting to play.

Comparing Statistics

Tracking the statistics of rounds, we can compare the amount of points each fish scores on average. I can adjust the point values that each fish gets so they are balanced and feel rewarding to play.
