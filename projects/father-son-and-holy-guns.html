The Internship

Father Son and Holy Guns is an action packed roguelite game being developed by Jumbo Jumps, a AA studio based in Bangkok, Thailand. I traveled to Thailand for a 2 month internship and helped test and balance the game for its demo and early access release. I got to experience working in a large studio of about 50, using weekly scrum sprints.

Testing and Balance

I worked closely with the lead designer to test the game for feel, bugs, balance and performance.

  • I played runs of both single player and multiplayer, playing all 4 characters to test all of their upgrades.
  • I tested for balance of the characters and reported if there were any builds or synergies that were overpowered.

Feedback That Got Implemented

Sister Lilith

Problem
Sister Lilith has a life steal ability that was giving too much health back any time she used a special attack, making her functionally immortal.
Change
I suggested decreasing the amount she healed, and nerfing the upgrades that reduce special cooldown. This made it so she was no longer able to infinitely heal by only spamming special moves.

Sister Kamara

Problem
Sister Kamara was immune when charging her special attacks, which made her very strong.
Change
I suggested that the length of the charge should be reduced, resulting in the attacks feeling more responsive and the immunity less impactful.

Lightning Effect

Problem
The lightning effect when used on a dash attack would create an overwhelming amount of visual effects that caused the game to lag.
Change
By reducing the amount of visuals for the effect on a dash attack, the lag was fixed.

Publishing on Steam

This is the first Steam game made by Jumbo Jumps, and I have experience publishing my own projects, so I was able to provide helpful insight on what it is like to publish a game on Steam.

  • I worked with the head producer to set up their Steam page.
  • I helped prepare a demo for the game, showing the production team how to add additional packages for a demo, which I knew from publishing MAGE.

Feedback for an International Audience

Even though the game is made by a Thai studio, they are targeting an international, primarily English speaking audience in Europe and the United States.

  • I provided feedback on how easy the game was to understand as an English speaker. The game supports English, but there were some descriptions that didn’t make sense through translation.
  • I helped the team iterate on the game trailer, advising on what American players would and wouldn’t respond well to, and suggested focusing more on gameplay and the power fantasy.
