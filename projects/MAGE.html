How It Plays

MAGE has very simple controls, using only one joystick and 3 buttons. You aim your wand with the joystick, fire projectiles in the direction you’re facing, move in the opposite direction, and Counterspell to parry enemy projectiles.

Designing Unique Characters

MAGE has 21 unique characters, each with a different kit and playstyle. As lead designer, I designed over half the roster and gave input on the rest. Every character has one of 2 styles of movement (in the air or off walls) and unique projectiles, and they all share the same Counterspell ability to parry projectiles.

Fire Mage

  • A simple character with a low skill floor and a high skill ceiling.
  • Very fast projectile, balanced by a high cooldown and lots of recoil.
  • Short dash on Counterspell to build momentum.
  • Very slow movement but low damping, so he can build up lots of speed.
  • New players enjoy his strong attack, while skilled players master his recoil and dash to compensate for his lack of movement.

Gravity Mage

  • Only moves bouncing off walls.
  • Gains a rock whenever he bounces off a wall (can have up to 3 at once).
  • Projectiles can be fired rapidly, but only if he has the rocks.
  • Rewards the player for good movement, and allows aggressive bursts.

Ghost Mage

  • Fires a boomerang projectile.
  • Shorter range projectile, but it can hit on the way back.
  • Can Counterspell his own projectile to shoot a charged shot.
  • Aggressive playstyle with a higher incentive to master Counterspelling.

Balancing Through Playtesting

I ran many testing sessions with friends, family and classmates to make sure no mage felt too strong or too weak. I collected feedback through testing forms to statistically compare how viable players thought each mage was, and used Steam stats from the demo and the full game to see how often each mage was picked and how often they won. For the most part the game was well balanced, but a few characters needed changes:

Wind Mage

Too strong

Problem
With the ability to redirect projectiles, Wind Mage could shoot players from around walls without ever being at risk of getting hit.
Change
Lowered his movement force so he couldn’t hide behind walls all game, and limited him to one projectile on screen at a time with a decent cooldown.
Result
The projectile is still very strong, but he can’t rapid-fire like other characters, making him a very tactical high-risk high-reward character.

Flower Mage

Too strong for new players

Problem
Her projectiles explode on walls, giving her a larger threat range, especially against newer players who haven’t mastered movement.
Change
Lowered her attack speed by a substantial margin, and made her projectile come back a little faster when destroyed.
Result
She can still use her secondary mechanic of bouncing off her own explosions, but is no longer overly oppressive to new players.

Moon Mage

Too weak

Problem
He has almost no gravity and can only move by jumping off walls. A cool concept, but incredibly hard to play, especially for new players.
Change
Increased the range he can jump off walls from, added a charge attack whose recoil grows the longer you charge it to get back toward walls, and gave his Counterspell one free jump in any direction to recover when stuck.
Result
Still one of the harder characters to learn, but now feels powerful and fun to play.

Adaptive Soundtrack

Listen on Spotify

MAGE features an adaptive soundtrack that I both wrote the music for and implemented.

  • 26 tracks in the main score (5 base tracks and 1 for each of the 21 characters)
  • 2–4 mage tracks featured in every battle

How it works

  • Each mage has their own instrument and music written in the main theme.
  • Their instrument is only played if that mage is alive in the round.
  • The music is not intended for all of the tracks to be listened to at once. Instead, it allows for a unique combination of music to be played for every unique battle in the arena.
  • Every track is played simultaneously, with only the specific mages in the round being unmuted. This allows for tracks to be interchanged seamlessly when a mage dies or a player switches their character.
